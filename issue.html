The issue

Gender equality is still unfinished

Women and girls make up half of the world's people, and half of its potential. Equal rights are a basic human right, and they also make communities more peaceful and better off. We're not there yet.

Why it matters

Half the world can't be left behind

When women and girls have the same chances as men and boys, everyone gains: economies grow, families are healthier, and societies are more stable.

According to UN Women, there is still a long way to go. To get there, the world has to end gender-based violence and make sure everyone has equal access to good education, health care, money and property, work, and a real voice in the decisions that shape their lives.

Gender equality is "the unfinished business of our time." António Guterres, UN Secretary-General

What needs to change

Six places to start

These are the areas the UN and UN Women keep coming back to. None of them can wait for the others.

01

End violence against women

Violence against women happens in every country. The UN calls it a pandemic: globally, a woman is killed every 10 minutes. Ending it is the first step to everything else.

UN · UNITE to End Violence against Women

02

Education and health for all

Girls and women need the same access to quality schooling and health care, including safe pregnancies, and protection from harmful practices like child marriage.

UNICEF · UNFPA

03

Fair pay and economic power

Women still earn around 20% less than men. Equal pay, and equal rights to own property and run businesses, help whole families and economies grow.

UN · UN Women

04

A seat where decisions are made

Women hold just over a quarter of parliament seats and about a third of local government seats. Leadership should look like the people it serves.

UN · Inter-Parliamentary Union

05

Laws that protect everyone equally

More than half of countries still don't have laws covering every key area, such as equal rights to marry or to start a divorce. Real change needs real laws.

UN · Sustainable Development Goal 5

06

Value care work

Cooking, cleaning, and caring for kids and elders falls mostly on women, unpaid. A fairer economy shares that work and invests in women and the people pushing for change.

UN Secretary-General's priorities

How we got here

A timeline of equal rights

Gender equality has been part of the UN's work since day one. Here are the moments that shaped it.

  1. 1945

    The UN Charter

    The UN is founded on respect for human rights for all, without distinction of sex.

  2. 1946

    Commission on the Status of Women

    A global body dedicated only to gender equality is created in the UN's first year.

  3. 1948

    Universal Declaration of Human Rights

    Equality between women and men becomes part of international human rights law.

  4. 1975

    First World Conference on Women

    Held in Mexico City during International Women's Year, followed by the UN Decade for Women.

  5. 1979

    CEDAW

    Often called an international bill of rights for women, this treaty defines discrimination and how to end it.

  6. 1985

    Nairobi conference

    About 15,000 activists take part. Many call it the birth of global feminism.

  7. 1995

    Beijing Declaration

    The world agrees that women's rights are human rights, and commits to specific actions.

  8. 2010

    UN Women is created

    Four UN offices merge into one body focused on gender equality and empowering women.

  9. 2015

    Sustainable Development Goal 5

    Achieving gender equality and empowering all women and girls becomes a global goal.

  10. 2025

    Beijing at 30

    A review finds progress, and a warning: nearly a quarter of governments reported a backlash on women's rights in 2024.

Things people say

Myth, and what's actually true

Myth

Gender equality has already been achieved.

Actually

More than half of countries still lack full legal protections, and the pay gap is still around 20%.

Myth

Feminism hates men.

Actually

It pushes back on gender roles that hurt everyone, men and boys included.

Myth

It's not my problem.

Actually

Empowering women boosts productivity and growth. Equal places are better off for everyone.
